# app: roadmap

The open points and the planned work of the models, their evaluation and the split for the capstone (step 21). How each model works, how it is judged, its numbers and the analysis behind the steps are in `docs/draft/helper/models/`, one file per model: `similarity.md` (S1, S2), `course.md` (C1), `topic.md` (T1), `difficulty.md` (D1, D2) and `prompt.md` (the prompt side). Note: the documentation will be updated and included in the final docs.

## The hand check of S1 (done 2026-10-30)

- **Done:** all 20 cards of `results/hand_check.md` are judged, 96 marks over the 173 pooled candidates. The chosen variant's hand-judged precision@5 is 0.68 (p@1 0.85), what S2 (step 23) has to beat, by about 0.08 on these 20 cards, or by less on the grown sample. The rule, the noise, the per-setting evidence and the improvement workflow: `similarity.md`.
- **The marks live in `results/hand_marks.csv`** (step 17), the 30 Jaccard candidates judged beyond the variants' among them; `hand_check.md` is the judging form. `results/` is tracked: its result rows are derived course data, its marks are hand labour.
- **The name:** the similarity model is S1 (M1 before), and M2 is S2; `evaluate.py`'s rows and `results/results.csv` say S1, and C1 for the map measures.

## 11. A hand-labelled sample (done)

- **Done:** the 56 unrated quizzes labelled easy, medium or hard by the demand on a learner (recall, apply, analyse), a criterion of their own since the authored levels are a quota of the writing, next to the 24 rated quizzes of decks 10 and 12 judged blind, which measure the criterion against the quota. `evaluate.py --part difficulty` writes the form `results/hand_labels.md` and keeps the labels in `results/hand_labels.csv` (card, level, judged, judge, note; read before the form is written again and written after it, tracked, `--judge` names who marked), scores every feature set's predictions on the judged quizzes by both label rules (accuracy, macro-F1, weighted kappa, the score's Spearman and the within-deck order, the share of a deck's pairs with different hand levels the score orders right: the `hand_` columns of `difficulty.csv`) and records the criterion against the authored levels (the `hand` row). The criterion, the numbers and the analysis: `difficulty.md`, "The hand-labelled sample".
- **Decided:** the feature set is `text` (`DEFAULT_FEATURES`; the same levels as `text+course`, the better order, no coupling to S1's vectors), the deck shares stay the label rule (the cut points call 52 of 56 easy), and the predicted groups stay a tentative order: by level D1 is within noise of always-easy (accuracy 0.61 against 0.57), by score it orders the quizzes (Spearman 0.66; within a deck 58 of 77 pairs right). That is what the recommender needs of it: a cold-start order of the extra quizzes that the learner's progress overrides (`difficulty.md`, "Conclusion"); whether the order helps is measured online (D3), not with more labels.
- **Open:** a sample of plain flashcards, later: the 732 flashcards carry no level, the larger gap for sequencing. A second judge would add value but is not available. The labels are the test set of D2 (step 12), of the option features (step 15) and of the calibration across decks ("Later").

## 12. Zero-shot difficulty tags, D2 (after step 11)

- **How:** zero-shot classification with an NLI model, the levels as hypotheses, no training (`difficulty.md`, "D2").
- **Judged like D1:** on the rated quizzes, with the same metrics and the deck shares on its scores, and on the hand labels of step 11 (`results/hand_labels.csv`, 56 quizzes). Nothing is fitted, so it needs no cross-validation. Compared with D1 first, then tried as an extra feature of D1.
- **The dependency comes with step 23:** `sentence-transformers` installs `transformers` and `torch`, so D2 adds only its NLI model (for example `facebook/bart-large-mnli`, 1.6 GB) to the same install. **Open:** the model and the template.

## 15. The quizzes' answer options in D1 (later)

- **Why:** D1 sees neither how many options a quiz has nor how close the wrong ones are to the right one, and the quizzes it predicts are exactly the many-choice ones (`difficulty.md`, "The data", answer options, and "What would improve the predicted groups").
- **Plan:**
  1. **The card carries its options:** the card record and the course table (new columns of `cards.csv`) carry `options`, `correct` and `answers`, so the models can read them; further columns come when the dataset grows. The viewer's parser reads them already, apart from the card (`CardsFile.quiz`: a `Quiz` with `options`, `answers` and `correct`), so no change of the viewer is needed: `export_course.py` writes them into the table, `glue/course.py` hands them to the card record in mode (b), and `evaluate.py`'s hand-label form takes them from the table, so it needs no viewer. The similarity index may take the option texts too, as a vectoriser variant measured like the others (the unrated quizzes agree least with their topics: 0.48).
  2. **D1's features:** the number of options, the number of correct ones (many-choice or not), the length of the options, the cosine of the wrong options to the correct one (the closest distractor) and the length of the per-option feedback. Never the order of the options.
  3. **Many-choice stays a feature,** not a model of its own (29 cards are too few); the hand-labelled sample judges the predictions.
  4. **Later, a second model:** correct or wrong per option (F2).
- **Done when:** D1's rows with the option features stand next to the rows without them in `difficulty.csv`, on the same cross-validation.

## 17. The card layouts measured with a fixed small k, with the evaluation's plumbing for S2 (done)

- **Done, the measure:** `evaluate.py --part similarity` records the within-topic overlap at k = 2 and k = 3 over the topics with k + 2 cards or more (with k + 1 cards every other card is a neighbour, and the overlap is 1 for any layout) for the model, the SVD start and random directions, with the lift over random ((layout − random) / (1 − random)) and the topic layouts' trustworthiness at the layout's k (defined for the 35 topics with 11 cards or more); the k = min(5, n − 1) column stays (`overlap_k2`, `lift_k2`, `overlap_k3`, `lift_k3`, `trust_cards` in `results.csv`; `evaluation.mean_overlap` and `lift`). The fixed k separates the layouts: at k = 2 the model keeps 0.81 of a card's neighbours, the SVD start 0.70, random 0.34, the lift 0.71 against 0.55 (`course.md`, "The numbers").
- **Done, the plumbing for S2:** the judgements live in `results/hand_marks.csv` (card, candidate, relevant, judged, judge, note; the Jaccard judgements merged in), read before `hand_check.md` is written again and written after it, so a removed variant or a changed sample loses nothing, and a candidate new under a judged card is tagged in the form; hand p@1 and p@3 next to p@5, and the paired difference of hand p@5 to the chosen variant with its bootstrap interval (`evaluation.paired_bootstrap`, 5,000 draws over the judged cards) as columns of `results.csv`; every variant's percentiles of the fifth neighbour printed and its minimum score set at its own 10th percentile (the `min_score` column); a variant is a factory of anything with `fit_transform` (`Variant`); the rows say S1 and C1 (M1 and M1-map before, the old rows renamed).
- **Done, the travel:** `evaluate.py` reads the course table, draws the random points itself (`evaluation.random_directions`, uniform on the sphere), takes the results folder as an argument (`--results`, default `results/` next to it) and travels with every train, `test_evaluate.py` with it. Without `hand_marks.csv` or `hand_labels.csv` and their forms in the results folder (the capstone) the hand columns stay empty, no form is written and one line says so; the hand-label form's quiz options come from the viewer's parser where the course file and the viewer are there. The rows that depended on the viewer's draw (every B0 row, the random layouts) were dropped and recorded again with the new draw.
- **Found:** with each variant at its own minimum, `min_df=1` is no longer the one significant loss: the fixed 0.1 had cut its weak one-off matches (its own minimum is 0.055); its difference to the chosen variant is −0.04 [−0.13, +0.06] (`similarity.md`, "The hand check", point 4).
- **Open:** the quiz options in the course table (step 15, point 1), so the hand-label form needs no viewer.

## 23. S2: sentence embeddings, hybrid with TF-IDF (after step 17)

- **Decided 2026-10-30:** semantic textual similarity with torch: `sentence-transformers` encodes each card's text once, `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` first and `all-mpnet-base-v2` as the quality check, and the hybrid sums S1's score and the embedding score, each normalised at its own minimum (step 17). A `fit_transform` wrapper plugs the encoder into `SimilarityIndex.fit` as one more variant of `evaluate.py`. Why, and the measures it was chosen over: `similarity.md`, "S2" and "The measures of the list".
- **Judged** through the pool-judge-compare loop (`similarity.md`, "Improvement workflow"): the new candidates judged by the rule first, then the paired difference to S1 with its interval, hand p@1 kept, and the misses tagged (lexical trap, shared dataset, generic term) so the gain shows where it was expected.
- **Grow the sample with it:** `HAND_CHECK_DECKS` at step 1 for decks 17 and 24 (70 cards, the interval about half of today's; step 1 keeps every judged card, step 2 would drop four of deck 24's), and deck 18 (AI Agents & RAG Systems, 42 cards) as the third deck, for transfer. 20 cards resolve about 0.08 and nothing smaller.
- **Dependencies:** `sentence-transformers` 6.2.0 with `torch` 2.15.0, 46 packages in all, none of them in the module env today (the versions, the sizes and the models' downloads: `similarity.md`, "S2").
  - **Where declared:** here, a dependency group `embeddings` in `modules/helper/pyproject.toml` (today: scikit-learn, scipy and the project), listed in `[tool.uv] default-groups` so the launcher's `uv sync --locked --check` keeps passing with it installed, after `uv lock` has updated `uv.lock`. In the capstone an extra (`app[embeddings]`) with the CPU index, so `uv sync` without it stays small and the model tests without torch still run on fixtures; a test that needs the encoder skips without it.
  - **Torch on Linux:** the capstone's CI and any Linux machine pin torch to the CPU index (`https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cpu`; with uv an `[[tool.uv.index]]` entry with `explicit = true` and a `[tool.uv.sources]` line for torch), since the Linux wheel pulls the CUDA libraries.
  - **Open:** the start-up cost: encoding the 1,064 cards at every start, next to the 1.7 s fit, or a cache of the embeddings as a derived file of the course.
- **Done when:** the hybrid's paired interval against S1 excludes zero on the grown sample without losing the first neighbour (hand p@1), with every variant's minimum at its own 10th percentile and the misses tagged. Then the draft roadmap's sentence holds: the better one provides the neighbours and the positions, so D1 is refitted (`--part difficulty`; its course features are the index's) and T1 and the map measures are recorded again (`--part topics`; step 17's fixed-k measure) in the same run.
- **Reuse:** the encoder is the content judge of the image texts and the request half of the navigation prompt (`prompt.md`).

## The offline evaluation

`evaluate.py [--part similarity|difficulty|topics] [--table data/course] [--results results]` measures the models of `app/models/` on the course table and appends one row per model and variant to the results folder: `--part similarity` S1's variants, the random floor B0 and C1's map (`results.csv`, and the hand-check candidates in `hand_check.md`), `--part topics` T1 (`topics.csv`), `--part difficulty` D1 (`difficulty.csv`, and the hand-label form `hand_labels.md` with the labels in `hand_labels.csv`); what each part measures is in the model's file, "How it is judged" or "The measurement". Without `--part` all three parts run. The rows note the course's decks ("decks 01 to 24"); older rows of the 48-deck course stay above them. It travels with the package (`uv run evaluate.py` in the capstone, into `results/` there) and imports nothing of the viewer.

- **The hand side stays in the workspace the trains come from,** where `results/` is tracked: the result rows are derived course data, the hand marks and labels (`hand_marks.csv` with its form `hand_check.md`, `hand_labels.csv` with `hand_labels.md`) are hand labour. The marks and labels travel with every train without their notes (step 24), so the hand columns are computed everywhere; the forms stay here (they hold card text): where a results folder holds the marks without their form, nothing is written back, and where it holds neither, the `hand_` columns stay empty. The columns are the same everywhere, so the files merge. Every row carries its run, the start time to the minute (`run`), and the standalone page shows the newest run per level; what the columns mean: `columns.md`.
